# Blended Families — 12-Session Bank

**For:** Claude Code
**Owner:** Zac
**Stream:** `blended`
**Audience:** Step-parents, remarried parents, and couples raising children from previous relationships. Salvation Army outreach.

Build as a stream inside the existing Life Group site. App behaviour, design, NLT handling, highlighting, PDF export and the leader/participant gate are specified in `marriage-life-group-sessions.md`.

---

## Differences from other streams

**Generic parenting material actively misleads this group.** Advice that assumes shared history, automatic authority and a couple who were together before the children arrived will make a step-parent's situation worse. This bank is written from the opposite assumptions and shouldn't be softened toward the standard parenting stream.

**The room contains different jobs.** A birth parent and a step-parent in the same marriage are doing genuinely different work with different licences. Where a question needs one or the other, the app should say so:

- **If you're the birth parent…**
- **If you're the step-parent…**

Build this as a per-question variant. Sessions 3, 4, 6 and 9 need it most.

**Run at 75 minutes**, same as the marriage stream. Countdown default 22 minutes.

**Confidentiality frame, on the gate and said aloud each session:**

> What's said here about our children, and about their other parent, stays here. None of them are in the room.

## The 12 sessions

### 1 — What we actually are

**Premise:** This is not a first family with extra people in it.
**Scripture:** Ruth 1:16–17

**Beats**
1. Ruth attaches herself to Naomi by choice rather than blood, and the Bible treats that bond as real. Chosen family is scriptural, not second-rate.
2. Blended families work on a different clock. The research consistently points to years, not months, before things settle — and most families are measuring themselves against a timeline that doesn't exist.
3. Naming the actual shape of your household honestly is more useful than aspiring to a shape it isn't.
4. **Landing:** how long have you given this, and how long did you privately expect it to take?

**Questions**
1. What did I expect this family to feel like by now?
2. What's actually true about how we function, said plainly?
3. Where am I measuring us against a family we're never going to be?
4. What's genuinely working that I don't give us credit for?

**Share:** one expectation you're letting go of.
**Commitment:** one honest sentence you'll say to your partner about where you actually are.
**Closing:** You are not behind. You're on a clock nobody told you about.
**Day 14:** Did you say the sentence?

---

### 2 — Built on a loss

**Premise:** Every blended family stands on something that ended.
**Scripture:** Psalm 34:18; Ecclesiastes 3:4

**Beats**
1. Whether it was a death or a separation, every child in this house has lost the family they were born into. That's true even when what came after is better.
2. Adults often experience the new marriage as a gain. Children frequently experience it as the moment the old family became officially unrecoverable.
3. Both are real. Your happiness doesn't cancel their grief, and their grief isn't a verdict on your marriage.
4. **Landing:** has anyone in this house been allowed to be sad about what ended?

**Questions**
1. What did each person in this house lose?
2. Have the children ever been given permission to grieve it, or has it been treated as settled?
3. What did I lose that I've never let myself name?
4. What would it change if I stopped treating their sadness as rejection?

**Share:** one loss in your house that's never been named out loud.
**Commitment:** one conversation where you let someone be sad without fixing it.
**Closing:** You can't build on a grief you're pretending isn't there.
**Day 14:** Did you let it be sad?

---

### 3 — The step-parent's actual job

**Premise:** You have influence. You do not have authority, and reaching for it early costs you both.
**Scripture:** Philippians 2:3–7

**Beats**
1. Paul describes someone who had every right to status taking the lower position instead. That's the posture, and it's a choice, not a demotion.
2. The most common step-parent mistake is disciplining too early. Authority in a step relationship is granted by the child over time; it cannot be assumed or announced.
3. In the early years the birth parent holds discipline. The step-parent builds relationship. Swapping those around is what breaks households.
4. **Landing:** who in your house is currently doing which job, and did you ever decide it?

**Questions**
1. *(Step-parent)* Where have I reached for authority I haven't been given?
2. *(Birth parent)* Where have I handed my partner a job that was mine to do?
3. What's the current rule about who corrects whom, and does everyone know it?
4. What would it take for me to build relationship this month instead of order?

**Share:** one job that's going back to the right person.
**Commitment:** the birth parent takes back one discipline responsibility; the step-parent takes on one relationship-building thing.
**Closing:** Warmth first, authority later, and only if it's given. There's no shortcut and everyone who tries one pays for it.
**Day 14:** Did the swap hold?

---

### 4 — Loyalty binds

**Premise:** A child who starts liking you may pull away precisely because they've started liking you.
**Scripture:** Luke 12:51–53

**Beats**
1. Jesus says plainly that he will divide households — father against son, mother against daughter. Scripture doesn't pretend families are simple.
2. A child who warms to a step-parent can feel they're betraying their other parent. The closer they get, the more the guilt bites, and the sharper the pullback.
3. So the rejection often arrives *after* a good stretch. Read as a verdict on you, it's devastating. Read as a loyalty bind, it makes sense.
4. **Landing:** the withdrawal after a good week is not failure. It's frequently the evidence that something worked.

**Questions**
1. Where have I seen a good stretch followed by a cold one?
2. What do I tell myself when they pull away?
3. *(Birth parent)* Have I made it clear the child is free to like my partner?
4. What would change if I stopped taking the withdrawal personally?

**Share:** one pattern you're now reading differently.
**Commitment:** one thing you'll say or do to release the child from the bind.
**Closing:** They are not choosing against you. They're trying not to lose anybody, using the only tools a child has.
**Day 14:** Did you manage not to take it personally?

---

### 5 — Two houses

**Premise:** You control one house. You have opinions about the other.
**Scripture:** Romans 12:18

**Beats**
1. Paul's phrasing is careful — *if it is possible, as far as it depends on you.* He builds in the fact that half of it doesn't.
2. Different rules in two houses is normal and children cope with it far better than adults do. What harms them is being made the messenger, the informant, or the battleground.
3. Most energy spent on the other household is wasted energy. Most energy spent on your own is not.
4. **Landing:** what are you trying to control that isn't yours to control?

**Questions**
1. What do I spend energy on that happens in a house I don't live in?
2. Have I ever put a child in the middle — asked, complained, or let them see too much?
3. What are our house's rules, and are they actually clear?
4. What could I release this month?

**Share:** one thing you're releasing.
**Commitment:** one clear rule set for your own house, and one thing you stop commenting on.
**Closing:** Run your house well. It's the only one you're responsible for and it's plenty.
**Day 14:** Did you stay out of the other house?

### 6 — The other parent

**Premise:** They are a permanent feature of your family, and no amount of wishing changes it.
**Scripture:** Romans 12:18; Matthew 5:43–45

**Beats**
1. Jesus' instruction to love those who are hard to love was never limited to strangers. It was always going to land here.
2. Co-parenting with someone you may not respect is one of the harder disciplines in Christian life, and it's rarely taught.
3. Your child is made of them. Contempt for the other parent lands on the child as contempt for half of themselves — even when it's unspoken.
4. **Landing:** what does your child hear in your voice when their other parent comes up?

**Questions**
1. What do I say — or convey without saying — about their other parent?
2. What's the actual grievance, and is any of it still worth carrying?
3. Where could I be more businesslike and less emotional in how we communicate?
4. What would my child need me to do differently?

**Share:** one thing you'll change in how you speak about them.
**Commitment:** one communication handled properly — brief, factual, no jabs.
**Closing:** You're not required to like them. You are required not to make your child pay for it.
**Day 14:** Did you keep your voice clean?

> **Leader note:** if someone describes a situation involving fear, safety concerns, or a court order being breached, this group is not the place to work it out. Stay with them afterwards and help them get to a solicitor or the relevant service that week.

### 7 — The marriage underneath

**Premise:** This marriage formed second, and it's the one under most pressure.
**Scripture:** Ecclesiastes 4:9–12

**Beats**
1. In a first family the couple predates the children. Here the children predate the couple, and every conflict has an older loyalty behind it.
2. A cord of three strands isn't quickly broken, says Ecclesiastes — but the strands have to actually be wound together, and in blended families that takes deliberate work.
3. Children in blended homes are unusually good at finding the gap between two adults. That's not malice; it's a search for the old shape.
4. **Landing:** are the two of you a unit, or two parents operating in the same building?

**Questions**
1. Where do the children find daylight between us?
2. What do we disagree about that we've never resolved in private?
3. When did we last have time together that wasn't about logistics or the kids?
4. What would it take to present as one to this household?

**Share:** one place you're closing the gap.
**Commitment:** one disagreement resolved privately, before it's tested publicly.
**Closing:** The children need you two to be solid far more than they need you to be right.
**Day 14:** Has the gap stayed closed?

---

### 8 — Money, wills and fairness

**Premise:** The practical layer nobody wants to discuss, and the one that causes the most lasting damage.
**Scripture:** Luke 12:13–15; 1 Timothy 5:8

**Beats**
1. Jesus refuses to arbitrate an inheritance dispute and warns about greed instead. He clearly knew what estates do to families.
2. Blended finances are genuinely complicated — child support, two households, unequal contributions, and children with different claims.
3. Wills are the sharpest edge. Families that functioned for twenty years have detonated over a document nobody read until after a funeral.
4. **Landing:** if you died this year, does everyone know what you intended and why?

**Questions**
1. What's actually fair here, and does "fair" mean equal or does it mean something else?
2. What do we not talk about because it's uncomfortable?
3. Have we made wills that reflect this family, and has anyone read them?
4. What resentment about money is sitting unspoken in our house?

**Share:** one thing you need to sort out.
**Commitment:** one practical step — a conversation, an appointment, a document started.
**Closing:** Sort it while you're alive and can explain yourself. Nobody gets that chance twice.
**Day 14:** Did you take the step?

> **Leader note:** this session raises real legal questions, especially about wills and estates. Say plainly that nobody in the room is giving legal advice and point people to a solicitor. Getting this wrong in a blended family is expensive in ways that outlast everyone in it.

### 9 — When it isn't working

**Premise:** Being the outsider in your own home.
**Scripture:** Psalm 142:1–4

**Beats**
1. The psalmist says no one is concerned for him, that he has no refuge. That's in the Bible, said to God, without correction.
2. Many step-parents live with a low-grade sense of being surplus in their own house, and almost none of them say it out loud.
3. Naming it isn't disloyalty to your marriage. Carrying it silently for years is what actually damages the marriage.
4. **Landing:** say it tonight. This is the room.

**Questions**
1. When do I feel most like an outsider here?
2. What have I never told my partner about how this feels?
3. What do I need from them that I've never asked for?
4. What's one thing that would genuinely help?

**Share:** as much or as little as you want to.
**Commitment:** one honest conversation with your partner about how this actually feels.
**Closing:** You've been carrying it quietly because you thought saying it would make you the problem. It won't.
**Day 14:** Did you have the conversation?

> **Leader note:** the hardest session for step-parents. Don't let the group rush to reassurance. Some people will say something they've never said, and the right response is to let it sit, not to fix it.

### 10 — A new baby

**Premise:** The child who belongs to everyone changes what everyone else is.
**Scripture:** Genesis 37:3–4

**Beats**
1. Genesis is blunt about what favouritism does to a family. Jacob's preference for one son sets off decades of damage.
2. A shared baby is often experienced by the adults as unifying and by the older children as confirmation that they're the leftovers of something that failed.
3. Half-sibling language matters. So does whose photos are on the wall, who's in the Christmas card, and who gets called "our" child.
4. **Landing:** do the older children believe they're as much yours?

**Questions**
1. What have the older children been told, and what have they concluded?
2. Where does my behaviour favour one child, even unintentionally?
3. What language do we use, and what does it signal?
4. What's one thing that would make an older child feel equally held?

**Share:** one thing you're changing.
**Commitment:** one deliberate act of equal claim — a photo, a phrase, a tradition, a conversation.
**Closing:** Children read the walls, the photos and the pronouns long before they read your intentions.
**Day 14:** Did they notice?

---

### 11 — Milestones

**Premise:** Weddings, graduations and funerals are where the whole structure gets tested in public.
**Scripture:** Romans 12:15

**Beats**
1. Rejoice with those who rejoice, mourn with those who mourn. Simple until you're deciding who sits in the second row.
2. Milestones force questions the family has been avoiding — who's introduced as what, who's invited, who pays, whose name is on the order of service.
3. The child at the centre usually wants everyone there and no atmosphere. Adults routinely make it about themselves instead.
4. **Landing:** at the next big day, what would the child actually want?

**Questions**
1. What milestone is coming, and what am I dreading about it?
2. What would the young person at the centre of it want?
3. Where would I be putting my own feelings ahead of theirs?
4. What can I decide now to make it easier for them?

**Share:** one thing you'll do differently at the next one.
**Commitment:** one decision made in advance rather than on the day.
**Closing:** It's their day. Behave in a way they'll be glad to remember.
**Day 14:** Has the decision held?

---

### 12 — What we're becoming

**Premise:** Ruth's chosen family ends up in the genealogy of a king.
**Scripture:** Ruth 4:13–17; Joshua 24:15

**Beats**
1. Ruth's story ends with the neighbours declaring a son born to her as Naomi's own, and that line runs to David and beyond. Scripture's most celebrated family is a blended one.
2. This household is not a repair of something broken. It's a real thing in its own right, with its own history starting now.
3. Traditions have to be built here, because you didn't inherit any together.
4. **Landing:** what will this family be known for, in twenty years, by the people who grew up in it?

**Questions**
1. What's ours — something this household does that no previous family did?
2. What do I want the children to say about this house when they're forty?
3. What's genuinely better here than what came before?
4. What one thing would I start this year?

**Share:** your one-line answer — *this family is a place where ___.*
**Commitment:** one tradition started, deliberately, this month.
**Closing:** Twelve months ago you were managing a situation. Export the year and read session one. You've been building something the whole time.
**Day 14:** Did the tradition happen?
