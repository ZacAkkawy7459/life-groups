# Parents of Young Children — 12-Session Bank

**For:** Claude Code
**Owner:** Zac
**Stream:** `parents-young`
**Audience:** Parents with children roughly 0–10. Salvation Army outreach.

Build this as a stream inside the existing Life Group site. All app behaviour, design, NLT handling, highlighting, PDF export and the leader/participant gate are specified in `marriage-life-group-sessions.md` — this file is content plus the differences.

---

## Differences from the marriage stream

**Sessions run 60 minutes, not 75.** This group is tired in a way the marriage group isn't. Sleep-deprived people stop absorbing at the hour mark, and a session that runs long is a session people quietly stop coming to.

| Time | Block |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:08 | Report back on last month's commitment |
| 0:08–0:15 | Catch-up |
| 0:15–0:23 | Message |
| 0:23–0:40 | Partner or personal time — drop off the call |
| 0:40–0:53 | Share |
| 0:53–1:00 | Commitments and prayer |

Set the countdown default to **17 minutes** for this stream.

**Sessions must survive interruption.** Somebody's child will wake up. Autosave already handles the data, but add a small **Where was I?** control that returns the person to the last question they touched, and make sure the countdown keeps running if the tab loses focus.

**Not everyone here is partnered.** Single parents will be in this group. Write every question so it works for one person answering alone — say "the other adult in your house, if there is one" rather than assuming a spouse. Sessions 1, 3 and 10 need the most care on this.

**Commitments should be smaller than the marriage stream's.** In this season a commitment that takes twenty minutes a week is realistic and one that takes an hour isn't. Put a line above the commitment card: *Make it small enough that a bad week can't kill it.*

## The 12 sessions

### 1 — Nobody warned us

**Premise:** Having children changed everything, including the things you didn't expect it to.
**Scripture:** Psalm 127:3–5; Genesis 2:24

**Beats**
1. Children are called a gift and a reward. That's true, and it sits alongside the fact that most people find the first years harder than anyone told them.
2. Both things are real at once. The Bible holds joy and lament in the same book without embarrassment, and you're allowed to as well.
3. What changed isn't just your schedule. It's your identity, your body, your friendships, and how you and the other adult in the house relate.
4. **Landing:** name what actually changed. You can't tend to something you won't say out loud.

**Questions**
1. What's changed about me since becoming a parent — the good and the costly?
2. What did I expect that turned out to be wrong?
3. What have I lost that I haven't let myself grieve?
4. What's one thing about this season I genuinely love and don't say often enough?

**Share:** one thing that changed that you weren't ready for.
**Commitment:** one small thing that gives you back a piece of yourself this month.
**Closing:** You are not doing this badly. You're doing something hard, in public, while tired, with no training. Start there.
**Day 14:** Did you get the thing back, even once?

### 2 — Tired

**Premise:** Exhaustion is a spiritual condition, not just a physical one.
**Scripture:** Psalm 127:1–2; Matthew 11:28–30

**Beats**
1. The psalm says God gives to his beloved even while they sleep. Read that as a parent and it lands differently.
2. Exhaustion makes you a worse version of yourself — shorter, meaner, more easily wounded. That's biology, not character failure.
3. Jesus offers rest to people who are already carrying something. He doesn't tell them to put it down first.
4. **Landing:** what would actually help? Not in theory. This week.

**Questions**
1. How tired am I, honestly, on a scale I'd be embarrassed to say out loud?
2. What do I do when I'm depleted, and does it help or just numb?
3. What's one practical thing that would genuinely make a difference, and what's stopping me asking for it?
4. When did I last sleep, eat and move like someone I was looking after?

**Share:** one thing that would help, said out loud so somebody else knows.
**Commitment:** one arrangement made — an hour, a nap, a night, a swap.
**Closing:** Ask for the help. Someone in this group has a free Saturday and no idea you need it.
**Day 14:** Did the arrangement happen, or did you talk yourself out of it?

> **Leader note:** exhaustion and low mood look similar and aren't the same thing. If someone describes a flatness that's lasted months, or says they can't enjoy anything, follow up privately afterwards and encourage them to see their GP. Don't diagnose, don't do it on the call, and don't let it pass unremarked either.

### 3 — Who does what

**Premise:** Most resentment in a house with small children is unspoken accounting.
**Scripture:** Philippians 2:3–4; Galatians 6:2

**Beats**
1. Somebody is keeping score. Usually both of you, silently, using different rules.
2. Paul's instruction is to look to the interests of the other. That requires knowing what those interests actually are, which requires asking.
3. The invisible work is the load nobody sees — remembering the immunisations, knowing the shoe size, holding the whole calendar in your head.
4. **Landing:** say the ledger out loud. It loses most of its power the moment it's spoken.

**Questions**
1. What do I do in a week that nobody notices?
2. What does the other adult in my house do that I've stopped noticing?
3. Where do I feel the split is unfair, and have I ever actually said so?
4. What's one thing I could take on that would genuinely lighten the load for someone else?

**Share:** one thing on your list that nobody sees.
**Commitment:** one job that changes hands, permanently.
**Closing:** Fairness isn't fifty-fifty. It's both of you believing the other is trying.
**Day 14:** Has the job stayed changed?

---

### 4 — The temper

**Premise:** Every parent loses it. Almost nobody talks about it.
**Scripture:** James 1:19–20; Ephesians 6:4

**Beats**
1. James is blunt — human anger doesn't produce the righteousness of God. Not "rarely". Doesn't.
2. Paul warns fathers specifically not to exasperate their children. The instruction assumes you're capable of it.
3. What you do after matters enormously. A child who sees a parent apologise learns something they'll use for the rest of their life.
4. **Landing:** what does your child see in your face when you've had enough?

**Questions**
1. What sets me off, and is it really about the child?
2. What do I do afterwards — apologise, justify, go quiet, pretend it didn't happen?
3. What did anger look like in the house I grew up in?
4. What's one warning sign I could learn to notice before I get there?

**Share:** one trigger you've identified.
**Commitment:** one circuit-breaker you'll use when you feel it rising.
**Closing:** You will lose your temper again. Apologise properly when you do — that's the part they'll remember.
**Day 14:** Have you had to use the circuit-breaker? Did it work?

> **Leader note:** this session can surface real shame, and occasionally something more serious. Say clearly that everyone in the room has lost their temper. If anything a person describes worries you — frequency, escalation, or a child being frightened — follow it up privately and help them find proper support. This group is not the place to work that out and you are not the person to assess it.

### 5 — What they're absorbing

**Premise:** Your children are learning faith and marriage by watching, not by being told.
**Scripture:** Deuteronomy 6:4–9

**Beats**
1. The instruction isn't a curriculum. It's talk about this at home, on the road, going to bed, getting up — in other words, in the ordinary hours.
2. Children absorb what's normal long before they can evaluate it. They're learning what an adult is by watching you be one.
3. That's frightening until you realise it also means the small ordinary things count. They're watching how you speak to a checkout worker, not just how you pray.
4. **Landing:** what's being modelled in your house that you didn't choose?

**Questions**
1. What is my child learning about God from how I live, not what I say?
2. What am I modelling that I'd rather they didn't copy?
3. What's one ordinary moment in our week that could carry something?
4. What did I absorb from my own parents that I've never examined?

**Share:** one thing you want them to catch from you.
**Commitment:** one ordinary moment you'll use on purpose — the drive, bedtime, breakfast.
**Closing:** You are not raising a project. You are being watched by someone who loves you, which is both the pressure and the gift.
**Day 14:** Has the ordinary moment held?

### 6 — Saying no

**Premise:** In this season, most of what you're saying no to is good.
**Scripture:** Mark 6:31; Ecclesiastes 3:1

**Beats**
1. Jesus pulls his people out of genuinely urgent work to rest. The need didn't go away — he left it there.
2. In this season the hard nos aren't to bad things. They're to good things: the roster, the invitation, the second job, the extra commitment.
3. A season has a shape. What's right for you now is not a permanent verdict on your capacity or your commitment.
4. **Landing:** what are you saying yes to out of guilt?

**Questions**
1. What am I currently doing that I've outgrown, or that this season can't carry?
2. What am I saying yes to out of guilt rather than conviction?
3. Who would I disappoint by stopping, and how much does that actually matter?
4. What would I do with the space if I got it back?

**Share:** one thing you're putting down for this season.
**Commitment:** one no, said to an actual person, before the next session.
**Closing:** Saying no to a good thing is not a failure of faith. It's how you stay standing long enough to do the next one.
**Day 14:** Did you say the no?

---

### 7 — Discipline

**Premise:** Discipline is formation, not punishment. They feel completely different from the inside.
**Scripture:** Proverbs 22:6; Hebrews 12:11

**Beats**
1. Hebrews says discipline is painful now and yields a harvest later. It's aimed at what a person becomes, not at settling a score.
2. Consistency matters more than severity. A predictable small consequence outperforms an unpredictable large one, every time.
3. Most discipline that goes wrong goes wrong because the adult is dysregulated, not because the rule was wrong.
4. **Landing:** what are you actually trying to grow in this child?

**Questions**
1. What am I trying to form in my child — and does my discipline actually aim at it?
2. Am I consistent, or does the response depend on how tired I am?
3. Where do the adults in this house disagree, and does the child know it?
4. What did discipline look like when I was small, and what did it teach me?

**Share:** one thing you'd change about how you handle it.
**Commitment:** one agreed, consistent response to the thing that keeps recurring.
**Closing:** You're not managing behaviour. You're growing an adult, slowly, at inconvenient times.
**Day 14:** Has the consistent response held up?

---

### 8 — Faith at home

**Premise:** Family faith without pretence is harder and better than family faith performed.
**Scripture:** Deuteronomy 6:7; Matthew 19:13–15

**Beats**
1. Jesus is interrupted by children and tells the adults to let them come. The interruption *was* the ministry.
2. Most Christian parents feel they're failing at this because they're comparing themselves to an imagined family that doesn't exist.
3. Short and real beats long and performed. A thirty-second prayer your child actually joins in beats a ten-minute devotion they endure.
4. **Landing:** what would faith at home look like if it fitted your actual house?

**Questions**
1. What do we currently do, honestly — and how do the children respond to it?
2. What am I avoiding because I think I'd do it badly?
3. What did faith at home look like when I was small, and did it help?
4. What's one thing small enough that we'd actually keep it up?

**Share:** one thing you're going to try.
**Commitment:** something under two minutes, most days.
**Closing:** Small and real. Everything else is decoration.
**Day 14:** How many days out of the last fourteen? Honesty over performance.

---

### 9 — Comparison

**Premise:** You are comparing your inside to someone else's outside.
**Scripture:** Galatians 6:4–5

**Beats**
1. Paul tells people to test their own work and take pride in that, rather than comparing themselves to their neighbour.
2. Every parent you're comparing yourself to is having a hard week they didn't post.
3. Comparison is particularly vicious in parenting because the stakes feel like a person rather than a performance.
4. **Landing:** whose approval are you actually chasing?

**Questions**
1. Who do I compare myself to, and what do I imagine they have that I don't?
2. Where does the comparison come from — a feed, a family member, a voice in my head?
3. What am I doing well that I never give myself credit for?
4. Whose opinion actually matters, and do they know it?

**Share:** one thing you're doing well.
**Commitment:** one source of comparison you're cutting off this month.
**Closing:** Do your own work. Take quiet pride in it. That's the whole instruction.
**Day 14:** Did the source stay cut off?

---

### 10 — The relationship underneath

**Premise:** The household runs on a relationship that currently gets whatever's left.
**Scripture:** Song of Songs 2:10–13; Ecclesiastes 4:9–12

**Beats**
1. Children are best served by adults whose relationship isn't quietly starving.
2. In this season the marriage or partnership gets the leftovers by default — the tired hour, the half-attention, the logistics conversation.
3. Nobody drifts back together by accident. It takes a decision, made when you'd rather sleep.
4. **Landing:** when did the two of you last talk about anything other than the children?

**Questions**
1. When did we last have a conversation that wasn't logistics?
2. What did we enjoy together before, and what would it take to do it once?
3. What does the other adult need from me that I've been too tired to give?
4. What's realistic in this season — not ideal, realistic?

**Share:** one thing you're putting back.
**Commitment:** one evening, one conversation, one walk. Small and dated.
**Closing:** Your children will one day leave. The person across the kitchen won't. Tend to that.
**Day 14:** Did it happen?

> **Leader note:** some people here are parenting alone. Frame this session as "the closest adult relationship you have" and make it explicit that a friend or family member counts. Don't let it become an hour that only half the room can enter.

### 11 — The village

**Premise:** You were never meant to do this in a single household.
**Scripture:** Ecclesiastes 4:9–12; Acts 2:44–47

**Beats**
1. The early church shared meals, homes and resources as an ordinary matter. Isolated parenting is a modern invention, not a biblical one.
2. Most of us find asking harder than giving. That's pride wearing the costume of not wanting to be a burden.
3. Receiving well is a skill, and refusing help can quietly deprive someone else of something they wanted to give.
4. **Landing:** who is on your list, and have you ever actually called them?

**Questions**
1. Who could I call at 9pm in a genuine emergency? Is the list shorter than I'd like?
2. What stops me asking — pride, logistics, not wanting to owe anyone?
3. What could I offer someone else that would cost me little?
4. What do I need that I've never asked for?

**Share:** one thing you need, or one thing you can offer.
**Commitment:** one ask made, or one offer made. To a real person, by name.
**Closing:** Somebody in this group needs exactly what you've got spare. Say it out loud and find out.
**Day 14:** Did you make the call?

---

### 12 — The house you're building

**Premise:** In twenty years, what will your children say this house was like?
**Scripture:** Joshua 24:15; Proverbs 24:3–4

**Beats**
1. Joshua's line is about a household with a direction — chosen, out loud, by the adults in it.
2. A house is built by wisdom and filled by knowledge, says Proverbs. Filled with what, though? That's the decision.
3. Your children won't remember most of what you did. They'll remember what it felt like to walk in the door.
4. **Landing:** in twenty years, what will they say this house was like?

**Questions**
1. What do I want my children to remember about growing up here?
2. What's currently true of our home that I'd rather wasn't?
3. What's one value we've never actually named out loud as a family?
4. What would change if we decided it this year rather than someday?

**Share:** your one-line answer — *this house is a place where ___.*
**Commitment:** one concrete step toward it, this month.
**Closing:** Twelve months ago you were surviving. You're still tired, and you're building something anyway. Export the year and read what you wrote in session one.
**Day 14:** What did the first step look like?
