# Money & Generosity — 12-Session Bank

**For:** Claude Code
**Owner:** Zac
**Stream:** `money`
**Audience:** Individuals, couples and households. Salvation Army outreach.

Build as a stream inside the existing Life Group site. App behaviour, design, NLT handling, highlighting, PDF export and the leader/participant gate are specified in `marriage-life-group-sessions.md`.

---

## Differences from other streams

**Do not assume a surplus.** The Salvos serve people in genuine financial hardship, and the standard church money course — which quietly assumes a middle-class household with spare income to redirect — will humiliate half the room. Every session in this bank is written to work for someone who is behind, not just for someone who is saving. Keep it that way.

**This stream is for individuals as much as couples.** Sessions 9 and 12 have household framing; every other session works for one person. Don't add couple assumptions elsewhere.

**No figures are ever shared with the group.** Unlike the other streams, the share-back here is always about a decision or a realisation, never an amount. Hard-code this into the Share tab for this stream: strip nothing automatically, but put a standing line above the copy box — *No numbers. Ever. Just what you decided.*

**Standing disclaimer, on the gate and on the PDF back page:**

> Nothing in this course is personal financial advice. It's a conversation about what money means and what you want to do with it. For decisions about products, investments, insurance, super or tax, see a licensed adviser or an accountant who knows your situation. If you're in financial difficulty, the National Debt Helpline (1800 007 007) is free, independent and confidential.

**Run at 75 minutes.** Countdown default 22 minutes.

> **Leader note for Zac specifically:** you're a chartered accountant and a registered tax agent, and this room will treat what you say as advice whether you frame it that way or not. Say at the start of every session that you're there as a member of the group, not as an accountant, and don't answer individual tax or product questions in the room — offer to talk afterwards or point them to someone. Also: you'll be the most financially comfortable person in most of these conversations, which will make people go quiet. Go second, and lead with something you got wrong.

## The 12 sessions

### 1 — What money means to you

**Premise:** You inherited a relationship with money before you ever earned any.
**Scripture:** Matthew 6:19–21

**Beats**
1. Jesus says where your treasure is, your heart follows. The order is the surprise — behaviour leads and the heart trails after it.
2. Nobody arrives at adulthood neutral about money. You absorbed a whole theology of it from the house you grew up in, and most of it was never spoken aloud.
3. Scarcity, security, status, secrecy — most people's money story is dominated by one of those four, and they can usually name it in about a minute.
4. **Landing:** what did money mean in your house growing up, and are you still living by that?

**Questions**
1. What did money mean in the house I grew up in?
2. What did I learn about money that I've never examined?
3. What does money make me feel now — safe, anxious, guilty, powerful, ashamed?
4. Where does my behaviour with money not match what I say I believe?

**Share:** one thing you inherited about money.
**Commitment:** one belief about money you're going to examine this month.
**Closing:** You didn't choose most of what you believe about money. You can choose what you do next.
**Day 14:** Did the belief hold up when you looked at it?

### 2 — Telling the truth

**Premise:** Most people don't know their own numbers, and the not-knowing is doing work.
**Scripture:** Luke 14:28–30; Proverbs 27:23

**Beats**
1. Jesus uses a builder counting the cost as an image people would instantly recognise. Knowing your position is assumed, not optional.
2. Proverbs says to know well the condition of your flocks. The instruction is to look, in detail, at what you actually have.
3. Avoidance is the most common money behaviour there is. Unopened envelopes, un-checked balances, an app you've stopped logging into.
4. **Landing:** what are you not looking at, and what is not looking costing you?

**Questions**
1. Do I actually know what comes in, what goes out and what I owe?
2. What am I avoiding looking at?
3. What would I find if I looked properly this week?
4. What's the story I tell myself about why I haven't?

**Share:** one thing you've been avoiding.
**Commitment:** look. One hour, this month, with everything open in front of you.
**Closing:** The number in your head is almost never the number on the page, and the gap is where the anxiety lives.
**Day 14:** Did you look?

---

### 3 — Debt

**Premise:** Debt is a maths problem wrapped in shame, and the shame is the harder half.
**Scripture:** Proverbs 22:7; Romans 13:8

**Beats**
1. Proverbs observes that a borrower is servant to the lender. It's a description of how debt feels, not a moral verdict on anyone in it.
2. Most debt is not the result of recklessness. Illness, job loss, family breakdown and low wages account for far more of it than overspending does.
3. Shame keeps debt secret, and secrecy makes it grow. Almost every good outcome starts with somebody telling someone the truth.
4. **Landing:** who knows your actual position? If the answer is nobody, that's the first problem to solve.

**Questions**
1. What do I owe, to whom, at what rate?
2. Who knows, and who doesn't? Why?
3. What's the story I've attached to it — about myself, not about the money?
4. What's the single next step, however small?

**Share:** a decision or a step. No amounts.
**Commitment:** one step — a call, a plan, a conversation, a hardship application.
**Closing:** Debt is not a verdict on your character. It's a balance with a path out of it, and the path starts with someone else knowing.
**Day 14:** Did you take the step?

> **Leader note:** somebody in this room is in real trouble and won't say so. Mention the National Debt Helpline (1800 007 007) out loud in this session — free, independent, and they negotiate with creditors. Also mention that most utilities and lenders have hardship programs that people don't know to ask for. Do not offer to look at anyone's situation yourself in the room.

### 4 — Enough

**Premise:** Almost nobody knows what number would make them feel safe.
**Scripture:** 1 Timothy 6:6–10; Philippians 4:11–13

**Beats**
1. Paul says he has learned to be content in plenty and in want. *Learned* — it's a skill acquired over time, not a temperament.
2. The famous line is that the love of money is a root of all kinds of evil. It's about the grip, not the amount — and people at every income level have the grip.
3. Contentment is not the same as passivity. You can be content and still ask for a raise.
4. **Landing:** what's your number? And do you believe you'd actually stop there?

**Questions**
1. What amount would make me feel safe? Would it, really?
2. What am I comparing myself to, and where does that comparison come from?
3. What do I already have that I've stopped noticing?
4. Where is wanting more actually appropriate, and where is it just appetite?

**Share:** one thing you have that you'd stopped noticing.
**Commitment:** one source of comparison you're cutting off this month.
**Closing:** Contentment is learned, which means you're not bad at it. You just haven't practised.
**Day 14:** Did cutting it off change anything?

### 5 — Giving

**Premise:** The conversation the church usually handles badly.
**Scripture:** 2 Corinthians 9:6–8; Mark 12:41–44

**Beats**
1. Paul says each should give what they've decided in their heart, not reluctantly or under compulsion. The decision is explicitly the giver's.
2. Jesus watches a widow give two small coins and says she gave more than the wealthy did. Proportion, not amount.
3. Giving under pressure or guilt produces resentment, and Paul rules it out directly. If you're giving because you'd feel bad not to, that's worth examining.
4. **Landing:** what have you actually decided about giving, as opposed to what you've defaulted to?

**Questions**
1. What do I give, and did I ever actually decide it?
2. What do I feel about giving — joy, obligation, resentment, guilt?
3. What have I been taught about tithing, and do I believe it?
4. If I could give to one thing and see it work, what would it be?

**Share:** one thing you're deciding about giving.
**Commitment:** decide something and write it down — an amount, a proportion, a cause, a change.
**Closing:** Decide it rather than drift into it. That's the whole instruction, and it applies whether the answer is more or less.
**Day 14:** Did the decision survive contact with the month?

> **Leader note:** if you're leading, don't name your own giving. Nothing shuts a room down faster.

### 6 — Work

**Premise:** What you do for money quietly becomes who you think you are.
**Scripture:** Colossians 3:23–24; Ecclesiastes 2:17–23

**Beats**
1. Ecclesiastes contains a genuinely bleak passage about the pointlessness of toil. It's in the Bible; you're allowed to feel that.
2. Paul's instruction is to work as if for the Lord, which dignifies ordinary work without pretending it's always meaningful.
3. Two failures sit either side: treating work as everything, or treating it as nothing. Both cost.
4. **Landing:** if you lost your job tomorrow, how much of your identity goes with it?

**Questions**
1. How much of who I am is bound up in what I do?
2. Am I paid fairly, and have I ever actually asked?
3. What am I sacrificing for work, and did I agree to that?
4. What would I do if money weren't the constraint — and is any part of that available now?

**Share:** one thing about work you're reassessing.
**Commitment:** one conversation — with a boss, a partner, or yourself.
**Closing:** Your work is real and it matters. It is also not you.
**Day 14:** Did you have the conversation?

---

### 7 — Planning

**Premise:** Prudence is not a lack of faith.
**Scripture:** Proverbs 21:5; Proverbs 6:6–8

**Beats**
1. Proverbs sends the sluggard to watch an ant storing food in summer. Planning ahead is presented as basic wisdom.
2. Some Christian teaching treats planning as distrust of God. Scripture doesn't — it treats it as ordinary competence.
3. A buffer is the difference between a bad week and a crisis. For most households it's the highest-value financial move available.
4. **Landing:** what would happen this month if the car died?

**Questions**
1. What would happen if a $1,000 bill arrived tomorrow?
2. Do I have a plan, or a vague intention?
3. What's one thing I could set up that would run without me thinking about it?
4. What am I planning for that I've never written down?

**Share:** one thing you're setting up.
**Commitment:** one automated or written thing — a transfer, a buffer, a plan on paper.
**Closing:** The system beats the intention every time, especially in a bad month.
**Day 14:** Is it set up, or still an intention?

### 8 — The things nobody sorts out

**Premise:** Wills, insurance, super and the paperwork of your own death.
**Scripture:** Luke 12:16–21

**Beats**
1. The rich fool builds bigger barns and dies that night. The parable isn't against planning — it's against planning as though you're the only person in the story.
2. Most Australian adults don't have a current will, up-to-date super beneficiaries, or any idea what their insurance covers.
3. This is the least spiritual-sounding session and one of the most loving things you'll do for the people who survive you.
4. **Landing:** if you died this month, what mess would somebody else have to sort out?

**Questions**
1. Do I have a current will? Does anyone know where it is?
2. Do I know what my super's death benefit does, and who it's nominated to?
3. What insurance do I have, and what does it actually cover?
4. Who would have to sort out my affairs, and have I made that easier or harder?

**Share:** one thing you're going to sort out.
**Commitment:** one document made, updated or located.
**Closing:** Somebody who loves you will have to do this on the worst week of their life. Do as much of it now as you can.
**Day 14:** Did you sort it?

> **Leader note:** point people to a solicitor for wills and to their super fund for beneficiary nominations. Don't let the group generate advice.

### 9 — Money in the household

**Premise:** Money is where control, trust and secrecy show up first.
**Scripture:** Ephesians 5:21; Luke 16:10

**Beats**
1. Faithfulness in small things predicts faithfulness in large ones, says Jesus. Money is the small thing that reveals the large one.
2. Financial secrecy in a household — a hidden account, an undisclosed debt, a purchase concealed — damages trust out of all proportion to the amount.
3. Control over money is a common way one person controls another, and it's often invisible from outside the relationship.
4. **Landing:** does everyone in your household know the actual position?

**Questions**
1. Who knows what, in my house?
2. Is there anything I've kept quiet about? What would it take to say it?
3. Who makes the decisions, and how did that get decided?
4. If I live alone: who would know my position if something happened to me?

**Share:** one thing you're bringing into the open.
**Commitment:** one conversation, or one thing disclosed.
**Closing:** Secrecy about money is rarely about money. It's about what you think will happen when you say it.
**Day 14:** Did you say it?

> **Leader note:** financial control is a recognised form of coercive control. If someone describes not being allowed access to money, not being permitted to work, or being frightened of a partner's reaction to a purchase, that's not a budgeting problem. Speak to them privately afterwards and help them contact 1800RESPECT (1800 737 732). Don't raise it in the room.

### 10 — When it goes wrong

**Premise:** Job loss, illness and hardship happen to careful people.
**Scripture:** Galatians 6:2; Philippians 4:19

**Beats**
1. Carry each other's burdens, says Paul — an instruction that presupposes some people have burdens too heavy to carry alone.
2. Financial hardship carries a shame that other kinds of trouble don't. People will tell their church about a cancer diagnosis and not about a repossession.
3. Receiving help well is a skill, and refusing it can deprive someone else of something they genuinely wanted to give.
4. **Landing:** if it went badly wrong, who would you tell? Anyone?

**Questions**
1. Have I been through it, and what did I learn?
2. What would I be most ashamed to admit, and to whom?
3. Who could I actually tell?
4. What could I offer someone else who's in it right now?

**Share:** whatever you want to. Nothing is required.
**Commitment:** one thing said out loud, or one offer made to someone else.
**Closing:** The church you're part of exists in large measure because people were poor and somebody showed up. Let it work in both directions.
**Day 14:** Did you say it, or offer it?

---

### 11 — Generosity that isn't money

**Premise:** You have more to give than you think, and less of it is cash.
**Scripture:** Acts 2:44–47; 1 Peter 4:10

**Beats**
1. The early church shared meals, homes and possessions as ordinary practice. Very little of it looks like a transfer of funds.
2. Peter says each should use whatever gift they've received to serve others. Skills, time, a spare room, a car, an afternoon.
3. People in financial hardship often feel they have nothing to give, which is both untrue and corrosive. Generosity is not a rich person's activity.
4. **Landing:** what do you have that costs you little and would be worth a lot to someone else?

**Questions**
1. What do I have that isn't money — skills, time, space, contacts, a vehicle?
2. What have I received from others that I've never passed on?
3. Where am I hoarding something out of habit rather than need?
4. Who could use what I've got, this month?

**Share:** one thing you can offer.
**Commitment:** offer it. To a named person, before the next session.
**Closing:** Somebody in this group needs the exact thing you've got spare. Say what it is.
**Day 14:** Did anyone take you up on it?

---

### 12 — What our money is for

**Premise:** Money is a tool with no purpose of its own. Yours needs one.
**Scripture:** Joshua 24:15; Matthew 6:21

**Beats**
1. Money accumulates a purpose by default if you never assign it one, and the default is almost always more of the same.
2. Joshua's line is a household making a declaration out loud. Yours has never made one about money.
3. A stated purpose changes small decisions more reliably than willpower does.
4. **Landing:** in ten years, what will your money have been for?

**Questions**
1. What is my money actually for?
2. What have I done with money in the last year that I'm glad about?
3. What would I want said about how I handled money?
4. What's one thing I could change this year that would move it in that direction?

**Share:** your one-line answer — *our money is for ___.*
**Commitment:** one concrete change, this month, that serves it.
**Closing:** Twelve months ago most of you didn't know your own numbers. Export the year and read session one.
**Day 14:** Did the change stick?
