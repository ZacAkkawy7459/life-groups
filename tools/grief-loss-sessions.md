# Grief & Loss — 12-Session Bank

**For:** Claude Code
**Owner:** Zac
**Stream:** `grief`
**Audience:** Adults some months or more past a significant bereavement. Salvation Army outreach.

Build as a stream inside the existing Life Group site. App behaviour, design, NLT handling, highlighting, PDF export and the leader/participant gate are specified in `marriage-life-group-sessions.md`.

---

## Read this before building or running anything

This stream needs more care than the other six put together. Build the safeguards below into the app itself — they are not decoration.

**What this is.** A group of people who have lost someone, sitting together, saying true things. Company in the dark.

**What this is not.** Therapy, counselling, or treatment. Nobody running this is qualified to process trauma, and attempting it does harm. The facilitator's job is to hold the room, not to heal anyone in it.

**Timing.** Suggest that people wait around six months after a death before joining. Acute grief needs one-to-one support, not a group. Put this on the Start tab, worded gently, with the alternative offered rather than a door closed:

> If your loss is very recent, this group may not be the right thing yet. Griefline (1300 845 745) and your GP are better places to start, and we'll still be here later.

**Facilitator.** Ideally somebody with bereavement training. If that isn't available, the leader notes in each session are the floor, not the ceiling — read them all before session one, not one at a time.

**Standing note on the gate, the leader view of every session, and the PDF back page:**

> **Support:** Griefline 1300 845 745 (12pm–3am daily). Lifeline 13 11 14, any hour. Your GP can arrange a mental health care plan for subsidised counselling. StandBy Support After Suicide 1300 727 247. Red Nose 1300 308 307 for miscarriage, stillbirth and infant loss.

**Four rules the facilitator states aloud at the start of every session, without exception:**

1. Nobody has to share anything, ever. Silence is a complete contribution.
2. Nobody gives advice, and nobody offers a silver lining.
3. What's said here stays here.
4. You can leave the call at any point without explaining.

Put these on screen at the start of every session in this stream, in both views.

**Sessions run 60 minutes.** Grief is exhausting. Countdown default 15 minutes.

**No commitments in this stream.** Every other bank ends each session with a commitment and a fortnightly check-in on whether it happened. That's wrong here — grief isn't a project and turning it into one adds a way to fail at it. Replace the commitment card with a **Something to carry** card: an intention, a question to sit with, a person to contact. Never a task, never checked up on.

The day-14 message becomes a single line in every session: *How are you this fortnight?* That's all it ever says.

**Watch for these**, and act rather than waiting for next month: someone who can't function months on, someone entirely isolated, someone whose drinking has escalated, someone who says they see no point in going on. Don't assess and don't diagnose. Stay with them after the call, help them contact their GP that week, and know that Lifeline (13 11 14) is there any hour. Tell the person you'll check in, then actually do it.

## The 12 sessions

### 1 — Permission

**Premise:** There is no correct way to do this and no timetable you're behind on.
**Scripture:** Psalm 13:1–2

**Beats**
1. Psalm 13 opens with *how long* asked four times, straight at God, with no apology attached. That prayer is in the Bible.
2. Grief has no fixed stages and no schedule, whatever you've been told. People move in and out of it unpredictably for years.
3. Whatever you're feeling — numbness, rage, relief, nothing at all — is a normal thing for a person in your position to feel.
4. **Landing:** you don't have to be doing this properly. There is no properly.

**Questions**
1. Who did I lose, and who were they to me?
2. What have I been told about grief that hasn't matched my experience?
3. What am I feeling that I think I shouldn't be?
4. What would it mean to stop grading myself on this?

**Share:** their name, and one thing about them. That's all, and even that's optional.
**Carry:** permission to feel whatever arrives this fortnight without arguing with it.
**Closing:** You're not behind. There's nothing to be behind on.

> **Leader note:** first session sets everything. Read the four rules. Go first when sharing, and keep yours short — long enough that people know you mean it, short enough that it doesn't become your session.

---

### 2 — Lament

**Premise:** The Bible has a whole vocabulary for this and the church mostly forgot it.
**Scripture:** Lamentations 3:19–23; Psalm 42:3

**Beats**
1. Lamentations spends two and a half chapters in unrelieved bleakness before it reaches the line about mercies being new every morning. The hope comes *after* the honesty, and it does not cancel it.
2. Roughly a third of the psalms are laments. Complaint addressed to God is a biblical form, not a failure of faith.
3. You are permitted to say the worst of it out loud. Scripture models it.
4. **Landing:** what would you say to God if you weren't being careful?

**Questions**
1. What would I say if I weren't being polite about it?
2. What have I not let myself say, and to whom?
3. Where has my faith gone since this happened?
4. What do I need God to hear?

**Share:** as much or as little as you want.
**Carry:** one honest thing said to God this fortnight, unedited.
**Closing:** The hope in Lamentations comes on the other side of the honesty. You can't skip to it and you're not meant to.

---

### 3 — The body

**Premise:** Grief is physical, and nobody warns you.
**Scripture:** Psalm 6:6; 1 Kings 19:5–8

**Beats**
1. The psalmist describes flooding his bed with weeping and wearing himself out. The body is in this text.
2. Grief disrupts sleep, appetite, concentration and immunity. People genuinely think they're going mad; they're having a physical response.
3. Elijah, at the end of himself, is given food and sleep before anything else happens. That's the order.
4. **Landing:** what has this done to your body, and have you told anyone?

**Questions**
1. What's changed physically since this happened?
2. How am I sleeping, eating, functioning?
3. What am I using to get through, and is it working?
4. What would help me look after myself this fortnight?

**Share:** one physical thing you didn't expect.
**Carry:** one small act of looking after your body.
**Closing:** Rest and food. It's what the angel did for the prophet, and it's not beneath you.

> **Leader note:** if someone describes drinking more, not eating, or not sleeping for extended periods, follow up privately and encourage a GP visit. Say it as ordinary care, not alarm.

---

### 4 — Anger

**Premise:** At them, at God, at the doctors, at everyone still going about their day.
**Scripture:** Job 3:1–3; Job 10:1

**Beats**
1. Job curses the day he was born, says he'll give free rein to his complaint, and speaks in the bitterness of his soul. God's later response never rebukes him for it.
2. Anger at the person who died is extremely common and almost never admitted — for leaving, for choices they made, for what they left unfinished.
3. Anger at God is not a loss of faith. It requires believing there's someone there to be angry with.
4. **Landing:** who are you angry at, and have you ever said it out loud?

**Questions**
1. Who am I angry at?
2. Am I angry at them? What for?
3. What have I not admitted because it felt disloyal?
4. What do I do with the anger currently?

**Share:** whatever you're willing to.
**Carry:** permission to be angry without deciding it makes you a bad person.
**Closing:** Job said worse than anything in this room and the book still ends with God on his side.

---

### 5 — The what-ifs

**Premise:** The loop that runs at three in the morning.
**Scripture:** Psalm 130:1–4

**Beats**
1. *Out of the depths I cry to you.* The psalm goes on to say that if God kept a record of sins, nobody could stand — and that with him there is forgiveness.
2. Almost every bereaved person has a loop: the thing they should have noticed, said, or done differently. It runs at night and it feels like evidence.
3. Hindsight invents a version of you who had information you didn't have. That version isn't real and you're being judged against them.
4. **Landing:** what's your loop, and would you convict anyone else on that evidence?

**Questions**
1. What's the thought that comes back?
2. What did I actually know at the time?
3. What would I say to a friend telling me this same story?
4. What would it take to put some of this down?

**Share:** as much as you want to. This one is hard to say out loud.
**Carry:** one what-if you'll try to hold more gently.
**Closing:** You're prosecuting someone who didn't have the information you have now. That case doesn't stand up.

> **Leader note:** genuinely heavy. Don't rush to reassure — let people say the thing before anyone responds. If someone's guilt seems fixed and disproportionate and has lasted months, that's worth a private conversation about seeing someone.

---

### 6 — Other people

**Premise:** They mean well, and some of what they say is unbearable.
**Scripture:** Job 2:11–13; Romans 12:15

**Beats**
1. Job's friends sit with him on the ground for seven days without saying a word, because they see how great his suffering is. That's the best thing they do in the entire book. Then they open their mouths.
2. People say terrible things out of discomfort — *at least*, *everything happens for a reason*, *they're in a better place*. Almost all of it is them managing their own unease.
3. Some friendships won't survive this. Others will come from unexpected directions.
4. **Landing:** what do you actually need people to do, and have you ever told them?

**Questions**
1. What's the worst thing someone has said, and where do I think it came from?
2. Who has been genuinely good, and what did they do?
3. Who has disappeared, and how do I feel about that?
4. What do I actually need from people?

**Share:** one thing that helped, or one thing that didn't.
**Carry:** tell one person what would actually help.
**Closing:** Seven days of silence on the ground. That was the good part. Most people can't manage seven minutes.

---

### 7 — When faith goes quiet

**Premise:** Psalm 88 ends in darkness. It's still in the Bible.
**Scripture:** Psalm 88:1–2, 18

**Beats**
1. Psalm 88 is the one lament that never resolves. It begins in trouble and ends with darkness as the writer's closest friend. No turn, no hope, no tidy finish.
2. Whoever assembled the psalter left it in. That's a deliberate decision about what people are allowed to bring.
3. Prayer often stops working after a death. Church can become unbearable. Neither means your faith is gone; both are extremely common and rarely admitted.
4. **Landing:** where has God been in this, honestly?

**Questions**
1. What's happened to my faith since this?
2. What can't I pray anymore, and what can I still say?
3. What's church been like, and have I told anyone the truth about it?
4. What do I still hold on to, even if it's very little?

**Share:** as much or as little as you want.
**Carry:** the permission to have a faith that's gone quiet without concluding it's gone.
**Closing:** The psalm that ends in the dark was kept. Somebody decided that people needed it available.

> **Leader note:** do not defend God in this session. Don't correct anyone's theology, don't offer explanations for the death, and don't let the group do it either. If someone says they've lost their faith, the right answer is *thank you for telling us*, not an argument.

---

### 8 — Firsts and dates

**Premise:** The calendar keeps arriving.
**Scripture:** Psalm 42:4; Ecclesiastes 3:1–4

**Beats**
1. The psalmist remembers going to the house of God with the festive crowd, and the memory hurts. Recalling good times is part of the pain, not a break from it.
2. Birthdays, anniversaries, Christmas, the date itself. The dread beforehand is frequently worse than the day.
3. These days can be planned for. Deciding in advance where you'll be and who with takes some of the power out of them.
4. **Landing:** what's the next hard date, and what's your plan?

**Questions**
1. What date is coming, and what am I dreading about it?
2. What happened last time one of these came around?
3. What would I want that day to look like?
4. Who could I ask to be with me, or to check in?

**Share:** the date that's coming.
**Carry:** one plan for the next hard day, and one person told about it.
**Closing:** Decide in advance. On the day itself you won't have it in you.

> **Leader note:** collect these dates. If someone names a date coming up, put a note in your phone and message them on it. It is the single most valuable thing a facilitator does in this stream and it takes thirty seconds.

---

### 9 — Carrying them with you

**Premise:** Not moving on. Carrying forward.
**Scripture:** Hebrews 12:1; 2 Samuel 1:17–27

**Beats**
1. David writes a lament for Saul and Jonathan and orders it taught to the people of Judah. He turns his grief into something the nation keeps.
2. "Moving on" is the wrong instruction and it's why so much grief advice feels like an insult. The relationship continues; its shape changes.
3. Talking about them, keeping their things, marking their days, telling their stories — these are not failures to progress.
4. **Landing:** how do you want to carry this person for the rest of your life?

**Questions**
1. How do I keep them with me now?
2. What am I afraid of forgetting?
3. What would I want other people to know about them?
4. What would honour them well?

**Share:** one story about them.
**Carry:** one act of keeping — write something down, tell someone, mark something.
**Closing:** Nobody is asking you to move on. Carry them. That's a different instruction entirely.

---

### 10 — When it's complicated

**Premise:** Some losses carry a weight that others don't, and they're the ones people speak about least.
**Scripture:** Psalm 34:18; Romans 8:38–39

**Beats**
1. The psalm says God is close to the brokenhearted and saves the crushed in spirit. It has no conditions attached about what kind of loss qualifies.
2. Some griefs come with a silence around them — suicide, a sudden death, a difficult or estranged relationship, miscarriage, stillbirth, the loss of a child. People often carry these for decades without ever saying them aloud.
3. Where the relationship was complicated, the grief is complicated too. Relief and love arrive together and neither is a betrayal of the other.
4. **Landing:** if you've been carrying something you've never named, this is the room.

**Questions**
1. Is there something about this loss I've never said aloud?
2. What do people assume, and what's actually true?
3. What's tangled up in this that isn't just sadness?
4. What do I need that I've never asked for?

**Share:** only what you want to. Silence is a full answer tonight.
**Carry:** one person you'll tell, if you're ready. Not required.
**Closing:** You've been carrying it alone because you didn't think there was anywhere to put it down. There is.

> **Leader note — the heaviest session in this bank, and the one most likely to need follow-up.**
>
> Read the four rules twice. Say plainly that nobody has to speak. Do not fill silences — count to thirty before you say anything.
>
> Do not let the group ask questions about circumstances. If it starts, stop it kindly and immediately.
>
> Have these ready before the session, and say them out loud: **StandBy Support After Suicide 1300 727 247**, free and available to anyone bereaved by suicide. **Red Nose 1300 308 307** for miscarriage, stillbirth and infant loss. **Griefline 1300 845 745**. **Lifeline 13 11 14**, any hour.
>
> Contact every person who spoke, privately, within two days. Contact the ones who didn't as well. If anything at all worries you about how someone is going, don't wait for next month — stay with them, help them get to their GP that week, and stay in touch.

### 11 — Living again

**Premise:** The first time you laugh properly, you may feel like a traitor.
**Scripture:** Ecclesiastes 3:4; John 11:33–35

**Beats**
1. There's a time to weep and a time to laugh, a time to mourn and a time to dance. All four, in the same sentence, in the same life.
2. Jesus weeps at Lazarus's tomb knowing what he's about to do. Grief isn't cancelled by knowing the ending.
3. Many people feel guilty the first time they enjoy something. As though joy dishonours the person, or means you've stopped missing them.
4. **Landing:** you're allowed to be happy again. It isn't a betrayal and it isn't the end of loving them.

**Questions**
1. When did I last enjoy something, and how did I feel afterwards?
2. What am I not letting myself do?
3. What would they have wanted for me?
4. What's one thing I might allow myself?

**Share:** one thing you've enjoyed lately, however small.
**Carry:** one thing you'll let yourself do without arguing about whether you've earned it.
**Closing:** Weeping and laughing are in the same verse. You're allowed both, sometimes in the same hour.

---

### 12 — What we carry forward

**Premise:** You now know something about this that most people don't.
**Scripture:** 2 Corinthians 1:3–4; Revelation 21:4

**Beats**
1. Paul writes about the God of all comfort, who comforts us so that we can comfort others with the comfort we ourselves received. The comfort is meant to travel.
2. Revelation's promise is that every tear will be wiped away. Not explained, not justified — wiped away. That's a different kind of hope from the one people usually offer.
3. You will be, for someone, the person who knows. That's not why this happened, and it's still true.
4. **Landing:** who has sat with you? And who might you sit with?

**Questions**
1. What do I know now that I didn't?
2. Who sat with me well, and have I ever told them?
3. Who might need me to sit with them one day?
4. What do I still need for myself?

**Share:** one thing you'd tell someone at the start of this.
**Carry:** thank one person who sat with you.
**Closing:** Twelve months ago you came into a room full of strangers carrying something you couldn't say. Export the year and read session one. You said it, and you're still here.

> **Leader note:** don't let the group end here without something continuing. Some of these people have found the only room where they can talk about this. Ask what they'd want next — the group continuing informally, a meal, a date in the diary — and make sure somebody owns it before the call ends.
