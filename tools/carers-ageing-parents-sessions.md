# Caring for Ageing Parents — 12-Session Bank

**For:** Claude Code
**Owner:** Zac
**Stream:** `carers`
**Audience:** Adults caring for, or beginning to care for, ageing parents. Salvation Army outreach.

Build as a stream inside the existing Life Group site. App behaviour, design, NLT handling, highlighting, PDF export and the leader/participant gate are specified in `marriage-life-group-sessions.md`.

---

## Differences from other streams

**People will miss sessions, and not because they lost interest.** A parent will be in hospital. Someone will be mid-crisis. Build this stream so a person can drop in at session 7 without having done 1 to 6 — each session must stand alone, and the Start tab should say so plainly:

> Miss one, come back. Nothing here builds on what you missed.

**Sessions run 60 minutes.** This group is depleted in the same way the young-parents group is, for the same reason.

**Attendance is often one person, not a couple.** Write everything for an individual.

**Standing practical note on the gate and PDF back page:**

> If you're navigating aged care, start with My Aged Care (1800 200 422) for assessments and services, and Carer Gateway (1800 422 737) for practical and emotional support for carers, including counselling and respite. Both are free. Dementia Australia (1800 100 500) is the place for anything dementia-specific.

**Countdown default 17 minutes.**

## The 12 sessions

### 1 — The turn

**Premise:** There's a moment you notice the roles have swapped, and most people can name it exactly.
**Scripture:** Exodus 20:12; Isaiah 46:4

**Beats**
1. Isaiah has God saying *even to your old age and grey hairs I am he who will sustain you.* Ageing is not something scripture ignores or tidies away.
2. There's usually a specific moment — a fall, a missed bill, a conversation that didn't make sense. After it, you can't un-notice.
3. The person you're now looking after is the person who looked after you, and both of you know it. That's the thing making it strange.
4. **Landing:** when did you first notice? And who have you told?

**Questions**
1. When did I first realise things had changed?
2. What have I taken on since, and did I ever agree to it?
3. What's hardest about this specific reversal for me?
4. Who knows what I'm actually carrying?

**Share:** the moment you noticed.
**Commitment:** tell one person the truth about what you're carrying.
**Closing:** You didn't apply for this and you probably weren't asked. Naming that isn't disloyalty.
**Day 14:** Did you tell someone?

---

### 2 — Honour, and what it doesn't mean

**Premise:** Honour your father and mother is a command about respect, not about limitless self-sacrifice.
**Scripture:** Exodus 20:12; 1 Timothy 5:8; Mark 7:9–13

**Beats**
1. Paul says anyone who doesn't provide for their household — especially their own family — has denied the faith. Real obligation, plainly stated.
2. Jesus criticises people who used a religious excuse to avoid supporting their parents. He takes the duty seriously.
3. And none of that requires you to destroy your health, your marriage or your children's childhood. Honour has never meant obliteration.
4. **Landing:** where have you confused honouring with never saying no?

**Questions**
1. What do I believe I owe my parent, and where did that belief come from?
2. Where have I said yes when I should have said no?
3. What am I doing out of guilt rather than love or duty?
4. What would honouring them well, sustainably, look like?

**Share:** one place you've confused honour with self-erasure.
**Commitment:** one no, said kindly, this month.
**Closing:** A carer who collapses helps nobody. Sustainable is the only kind that's actually available.
**Day 14:** Did you say the no?

---

### 3 — Guilt

**Premise:** Whatever you're doing, it will feel like not enough.
**Scripture:** Psalm 103:13–14

**Beats**
1. The psalm says God remembers we are dust — knows our frame, knows our limits, and doesn't hold them against us.
2. Carer guilt is nearly universal and almost entirely unrelated to how much the carer is actually doing. The people doing most usually feel it worst.
3. Some guilt is information — a thing you genuinely should do. Most of it isn't. Learning to tell them apart is the skill.
4. **Landing:** is this guilt telling you to do something, or just taxing you for being finite?

**Questions**
1. What do I feel guilty about?
2. Is that guilt asking me to do something specific, or is it just noise?
3. What would I say to a friend in my position?
4. What am I doing that I never give myself any credit for?

**Share:** one thing you're doing well.
**Commitment:** name one guilt as noise, and let it go for a month.
**Closing:** He knows what you're made of. He isn't the one keeping the account you think you're failing.
**Day 14:** Did the guilt stay put down?

---

### 4 — The practical stack

**Premise:** Power of attorney, aged care, the house, the car, the money. Nobody enjoys it and everybody needs it done.
**Scripture:** Proverbs 21:5; Luke 14:28

**Beats**
1. Proverbs says the plans of the diligent lead to profit. Blunt and unromantic, which suits this session.
2. The paperwork is far easier while a parent still has capacity. Every month of delay narrows the options, sometimes permanently.
3. These conversations feel like a betrayal to start, and like a gift within a year.
4. **Landing:** what's the one document that would make everything else easier?

**Questions**
1. What's actually in place — enduring power of attorney, guardianship, a will, an aged care assessment?
2. What's the conversation I've been avoiding?
3. What don't I know that I'd need to know in an emergency next week?
4. What's the single next step?

**Share:** one thing you're sorting.
**Commitment:** one call made, one form started, one conversation opened.
**Closing:** Do it while they can still tell you what they want. That window closes without warning.
**Day 14:** Did the step happen?

> **Leader note:** point people to My Aged Care (1800 200 422) for assessments and to a solicitor for enduring power of attorney and guardianship documents. Rules differ by state. Don't let the group generate legal advice.

### 5 — Siblings

**Premise:** One of you is doing most of it, and everyone has a view.
**Scripture:** Romans 12:18; Luke 10:38–42

**Beats**
1. Martha is doing all the work and says so, out loud, with some heat. Scripture records the complaint rather than tidying it away.
2. In most families the care falls unevenly — usually to whoever lives closest, or whoever says no least easily.
3. The sibling who does least often has the strongest opinions, and childhood dynamics come roaring back the moment a parent declines.
4. **Landing:** have you ever actually asked, specifically, for what you need? Or just hoped they'd notice?

**Questions**
1. How is the load actually split, and how did that happen?
2. What do I resent, and have I ever said it directly?
3. What am I assuming about my siblings that I've never checked?
4. What specific thing could I ask for — a weekend, a task, a phone call a week?

**Share:** one thing you're going to ask for.
**Commitment:** make the ask. Specific, to a named sibling, this month.
**Closing:** Vague resentment changes nothing. A specific request occasionally does.
**Day 14:** Did you ask, and what happened?

---

### 6 — Dementia

**Premise:** Grieving somebody who is still in the room.
**Scripture:** Psalm 139:1–6; Isaiah 49:15–16

**Beats**
1. The psalm says God knows us completely — before a word is on our tongue. A person's being known does not depend on their remembering.
2. Dementia produces a grief that has no funeral and no permission. You lose someone slowly, in public, while still doing their washing.
3. The person is still there. Not as they were, and not gone.
4. **Landing:** what have you lost already that nobody has acknowledged?

**Questions**
1. What have I already lost of this person?
2. What's still there that I can still reach?
3. What's hardest — the behaviour, the grief, the exhaustion, or being unrecognised?
4. What would help me hold on to who they were?

**Share:** one thing about who they were.
**Commitment:** one act of remembering — write something down, tell a grandchild, find a photo.
**Closing:** They are still fully known, whatever they can and can't hold on to. So are you.
**Day 14:** Did you write it down?

> **Leader note:** Dementia Australia (1800 100 500) runs a free national helpline and carer education. Mention it out loud in this session.

---

### 7 — Your own household

**Premise:** The people you live with are absorbing this too.
**Scripture:** Galatians 6:2 and 6:5; Mark 12:31

**Beats**
1. Paul says carry each other's burdens and, a few verses later, that each should carry their own load. Both are true; the skill is knowing which is which.
2. Care for a parent spills into a marriage and onto children — cancelled plans, short tempers, a parent who's physically present and mentally elsewhere.
3. The people closest to you often get the worst of you and complain the least, which is exactly how resentment builds unseen.
4. **Landing:** what has this cost the people who live with you, and have you ever said sorry for it?

**Questions**
1. What has this cost my household?
2. What have I stopped noticing about the people I live with?
3. What do they need from me that I haven't had left to give?
4. What's one thing I could protect for them?

**Share:** one thing you're protecting.
**Commitment:** one conversation with someone in your household about what this has cost them.
**Closing:** They're carrying this with you, mostly silently. Say that you've noticed.
**Day 14:** Did you have the conversation?

---

### 8 — When they weren't a good parent

**Premise:** Some of you are caring for someone who hurt you.
**Scripture:** Psalm 62:8; Romans 12:18

**Beats**
1. The psalm says pour out your heart before God — he's a refuge. There's no requirement to tidy the story up first.
2. Caring for a parent who was absent, cruel or unsafe is a genuinely different job, and almost nothing written for carers acknowledges it.
3. Honour does not require pretending. It does not require unlimited access, and it does not require you to be the one who provides the care.
4. **Landing:** you are allowed to set limits. Tonight is a room where that can be said.

**Questions**
1. What's the real history here?
2. What am I doing out of obligation, and what is it costing me?
3. What limits would I need for this to be survivable?
4. Who supports me in this specifically — and if nobody does, who could?

**Share:** only what you want to. Silence is a full answer tonight.
**Commitment:** one limit set, or one support sought.
**Closing:** Doing this at all, in these circumstances, is not a small thing. Nobody outside this room will understand what it costs you.
**Day 14:** How are you? One line is enough.

> **Leader note — read before running this session.** This will surface real histories, including abuse. Say at the start that nobody has to share. Do not press, do not ask follow-up questions, and do not let the group offer advice or theology about forgiveness — that is exactly the wrong response and it does damage. Your only jobs are to let it be said and to make sure the person isn't alone with it afterwards. Have counselling options ready before the session: Carer Gateway (1800 422 737) provides free counselling, and 1800RESPECT (1800 737 732) covers abuse. Follow up privately in the week with anyone who spoke, and anyone who obviously didn't.

### 9 — Your own health

**Premise:** Carers get sick. This isn't a noble side-effect; it's the main occupational risk.
**Scripture:** Mark 6:31; 1 Kings 19:5–8

**Beats**
1. Elijah, at the end of himself, is met by an angel who feeds him and lets him sleep. Twice, before any conversation about his calling.
2. Jesus takes his people away to rest while the need is still standing right there. The need did not go away; he left it.
3. Carers postpone their own appointments, their own scans, their own sleep. The statistics on carer health are not good and they are not accidental.
4. **Landing:** when did you last do something for your own body?

**Questions**
1. What have I postponed for myself — appointments, sleep, exercise, the thing I keep meaning to check?
2. How am I actually going, physically and mentally?
3. What do I do to cope, and is it working or just numbing?
4. What's one thing I'd book if I took myself seriously?

**Share:** one thing you're booking.
**Commitment:** book it. An appointment, a scan, a day off, a night's proper sleep.
**Closing:** Rest and food, before anything else. That's what the angel did, and it's the right order.
**Day 14:** Did you go?

> **Leader note:** carer burnout and depression are common and easily mistaken for tiredness. If someone describes a flatness that's lasted months, or can't identify anything they still enjoy, follow up privately and encourage a GP visit. Carer Gateway (1800 422 737) offers free counselling and respite. If you're ever concerned for someone's safety, don't leave it until next month — stay with them, help them contact their GP, and Lifeline (13 11 14) is available any hour.

### 10 — Grieving before the death

**Premise:** You are already mourning, and there's no ceremony for it.
**Scripture:** Ecclesiastes 3:1–4; Psalm 90:12

**Beats**
1. Ecclesiastes names a time to mourn as one season among others. It doesn't specify that the death must have happened first.
2. Anticipatory grief is real and largely unrecognised. You're grieving someone in front of you, which feels disloyal and looks like nothing from outside.
3. The psalm asks God to teach us to number our days. Knowing time is short is painful and can also be clarifying.
4. **Landing:** what do you want to have said before you can't?

**Questions**
1. What am I already grieving?
2. What do I want to say to them that I haven't?
3. What do I want to ask them while I still can?
4. What would I regret not doing?

**Share:** one thing you want to say or ask.
**Commitment:** say it, or ask it, this month.
**Closing:** There's a window. It's open now and it won't be indefinitely.
**Day 14:** Did you say it?

---

### 11 — The last stretch

**Premise:** Being present when there's nothing left to fix.
**Scripture:** John 19:26–27; Psalm 23:4

**Beats**
1. From the cross, Jesus arranges for his mother's care. In the worst hour available to anyone, the practical duty to a parent still mattered.
2. Near the end, the job changes from managing to accompanying. Most carers find that transition harder than the work itself.
3. Presence is enough. Sitting in the room, saying nothing useful, is the thing people remember having done.
4. **Landing:** what would it mean to stop trying to fix it and just be there?

**Questions**
1. Am I still trying to solve something that can't be solved?
2. What does being present look like, practically, this month?
3. What do I want to have done, when I look back?
4. What am I frightened of about the end?

**Share:** as much as you want to.
**Commitment:** one visit with no agenda. Nothing to fix, nothing to organise.
**Closing:** You don't need the right words. You need to be in the room.
**Day 14:** Did you go with nothing to fix?

---

### 12 — After, and being carried yourself

**Premise:** What you'll carry, and who carries you.
**Scripture:** 2 Corinthians 1:3–4; Revelation 21:4

**Beats**
1. Paul writes about being comforted so that we can comfort others with what we received. The comfort travels onward.
2. When it ends, carers often report an emptiness they weren't expecting — the role stops as well as the person.
3. You will know things about this that nobody who hasn't done it knows. Somebody five years behind you is going to need exactly that.
4. **Landing:** who has carried you? And who could you carry?

**Questions**
1. What will I take from this that I didn't expect?
2. Who has carried me well, and have I ever thanked them?
3. Who's five years behind me and could use what I know?
4. What do I need now, for myself?

**Share:** one thing you've learned that someone else will need.
**Commitment:** thank someone who carried you, or offer to carry someone else.
**Closing:** Twelve months ago you were doing this alone. Export the year and read session one.
**Day 14:** Did you make the call?
